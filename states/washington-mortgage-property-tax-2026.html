How Washington Property Taxes Impact Your 2026 Mortgage

Washington State combines 0% personal state wage income tax with a reasonable ~0.84% average effective property tax rate in 2026—making Seattle, Bellevue, and Tacoma far more property-tax friendly than Texas (1.63%) or Illinois (2.08%). Washington's constitution limits regular property tax levies to 1% of true market value (with voter-approved local school levies adding modest increments), and homeowners insurance stays relatively affordable (~$1,750/yr on a $500k home) compared to Gulf Coast states.

Real $500,000 Monthly Payment Breakdown in Washington

On a $500,000 home in Washington at 6.5% APR with 20% down, property taxes (~$350/month) and insurance (~$146/month) keep total monthly PITI near ~$3,024/month—nearly $440/month cheaper than Texas! With 10% down, adding ~$244/month in PMI lifts initial monthly payments to ~$3,584/month until LTV reaches 80% around Month 96. Note that when selling a home in Washington, sellers pay Washington's graduated Real Estate Excise Tax (REET, 1.1% up to 3.0% on $3M+ homes), though residential real estate is 100% EXEMPT from Washington's 7% capital gains tax.

Frequently Asked Questions: Washington Mortgage & Property Taxes

How much are property taxes on a $500,000 house in Washington in 2026?

At the 0.84% statewide average, property taxes on a $500,000 home average roughly $4,200/year (~$350/month)—though King County (Seattle/Bellevue) averages slightly higher around 0.90%–1.0%.

Does Washington's 7% capital gains tax apply when I sell my house?

No. All real estate sales (both primary homes and rental properties) are 100% exempt from Washington's capital gains excise tax.

How much is a $500,000 mortgage in Washington with 20% down?

At 6.5% APR, total monthly PITI averages ~$3,024/month ($2,528 P&I + $350 property tax + $146 insurance).

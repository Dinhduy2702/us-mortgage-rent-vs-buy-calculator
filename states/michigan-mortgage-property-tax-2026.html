How Michigan Property Taxes Impact Your 2026 Mortgage

Michigan averages a ~1.32% effective property tax rate in 2026, governed by Proposal A—which caps annual Taxable Value increases for existing homeowners at 5% or inflation (CPI), whichever is lower, AND exempts primary residences from up to 18 mills of local school operating taxes via the Principal Residence Exemption (PRE). However, Michigan buyers MUST beware the famous 'Proposal A Uncapping': the year after you buy a house, Taxable Value resets (uncaps) to 50% of current market value!

Real $500,000 Monthly Payment Breakdown in Michigan

On a $500,000 home in Ann Arbor, Grand Rapids, or Metro Detroit at 6.5% APR with 20% down, property taxes average ~$550/month ($6,600/year) and insurance runs ~$167/month—putting total monthly PITI near ~$3,245/month, or ~$3,805/month with 10% down including PMI. Never trust the seller's current tax bill on Zillow in Michigan: if the seller owned the home for 15 years under Proposal A, your Year-2 property taxes after uncapping can jump 40%–80%!

Frequently Asked Questions: Michigan Mortgage & Property Taxes

Why do Michigan property taxes jump the year after I buy a house?

Under Michigan Proposal A, the seller's taxable value was capped at inflation (<=5%/yr). When you buy the home, the taxable value 'uncaps' to 50% of current market value the following year—then gets re-capped for you going forward.

How much are property taxes on a $500,000 Michigan home in 2026?

At the 1.32% statewide effective average (with the Principal Residence Exemption), property taxes run roughly $6,600/year (~$550/month).

What is Michigan's Principal Residence Exemption (PRE)?

The PRE exempts owner-occupied primary homes from up to 18 mills of local school operating tax, cutting your property tax bill by roughly 30%–40% compared to a second home or rental.

How Massachusetts Property Taxes Impact Your 2026 Mortgage

Massachusetts averages a moderate ~1.12% effective property tax rate in 2026, governed by Proposition 2½—a statewide law capping total municipal property tax levy increases at 2.5% per year unless voters pass an override. Crucially for urban buyers, Boston, Cambridge, Somerville, and Brookline offer a massive Residential Exemption for owner-occupied primary residences that can slash annual property tax bills by $3,000 to $4,000+/year!

Real $500,000 Monthly Payment Breakdown in Massachusetts

On a $500,000 home in Massachusetts at 6.5% APR with 20% down, property taxes average ~$467/month ($5,600/year) and insurance runs ~$158/month, putting total monthly PITI near ~$3,153/month—or ~$3,713/month with 10% down including PMI. Because Greater Boston single-family prices frequently sit between $750,000 and $1,100,000+, combining Massachusetts's 5.0% flat income tax with the city residential exemption gives owner-occupants inside Boston/Cambridge a notable edge over suburban towns without residential exemptions.

Frequently Asked Questions: Massachusetts Mortgage & Property Taxes

How much are property taxes on a $500,000 home in Massachusetts in 2026?

At the 1.12% statewide average, property taxes run roughly $5,600/year (~$467/month)—and significantly less inside Boston or Cambridge after the owner-occupied Residential Exemption.

What is Massachusetts Proposition 2½?

Prop 2½ limits a Massachusetts city or town's total property tax levy growth to 2.5% per year (plus new construction), protecting homeowners from sudden tax spikes.

How much is a $500,000 mortgage in Massachusetts with 10% down?

At 6.5% APR, expect roughly $3,713/month initially ($2,844 P&I + $467 property tax + $158 insurance + $244 PMI), dropping to ~$3,469/month once PMI falls off at 80% LTV.

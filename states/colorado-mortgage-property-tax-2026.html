How Colorado Property Taxes Impact Your 2026 Mortgage

Colorado boasts one of the lowest effective property tax rates in the United States in 2026 at approximately 0.49%—less than one-fourth of New Jersey's 2.23% and less than one-third of Texas's 1.63%. Paired with Colorado's low 4.4% flat state income tax, Denver, Boulder, and Colorado Springs homeowners enjoy ultra-low property tax escrow bills, though Front Range hailstorms push homeowners insurance higher (~$2,650/year on a $500k home).

Frequently Asked Questions: Colorado Mortgage & Property Taxes

Why are Colorado property taxes so low compared to Texas or Illinois?

Colorado uses a low residential assessment rate (~6.7%–7.0% of market value) multiplied by local mill levies, keeping the statewide effective rate near 0.49%.

How much is a $500,000 mortgage per month in Colorado in 2026?

With 20% down at 6.5% APR, total monthly PITI averages just ~$2,953/month ($2,528 P&I + $204 property tax + $221 insurance).

What is a Colorado Metro District tax?

Developers of newer Colorado subdivisions often form Metropolitan Districts to finance roads and utilities via bonds repaid through extra property tax mill levies—always check the property's exact mill levy before buying.

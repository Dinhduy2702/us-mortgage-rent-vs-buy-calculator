How Georgia Property Taxes Impact Your 2026 Mortgage

Georgia delivers one of the most affordable property tax profiles in the Southeast in 2026, averaging roughly 0.83% effective rate statewide across Metro Atlanta, Savannah, and Athens. Georgia assesses residential real estate at 40% of fair market value before multiplying by local county/school millage rates, and voters recently approved a statewide floating homestead exemption cap (HB 581) tying annual homestead assessment increases to inflation in participating counties.

Real $500,000 Monthly Payment Breakdown in Georgia

On a $500,000 home in Atlanta or suburban Cobb, Gwinnett, or Forsyth County at 6.5% APR with 20% down, property tax averages just ~$346/month ($4,150/year) and insurance runs ~$183/month—putting total all-in PITI near ~$3,057/month, or ~$3,617/month with 10% down including PMI. Notably, several Metro Atlanta counties (such as Cobb and Cherokee) grant massive senior school-tax exemptions at age 62+, making Georgia exceptionally attractive for long-term homeowners.

Frequently Asked Questions: Georgia Mortgage & Property Taxes

How much are property taxes on a $500,000 home in Georgia (Atlanta) in 2026?

At Georgia's 0.83% effective average, property taxes on a $500,000 home run roughly $4,150/year (~$346/month), though City of Atlanta / Fulton County bills can run closer to 1.0%–1.1%.

When is the deadline to file a Georgia Homestead Exemption?

Most Georgia counties require you to own and occupy the home on January 1 and file your Homestead Exemption application by April 1.

How North Carolina Property Taxes Impact Your 2026 Mortgage

North Carolina combines a low ~0.73% average effective property tax rate with a low 4.25% flat state income tax in 2026—explaining why Charlotte, Raleigh-Durham, and Cary remain among the fastest-growing housing markets in America. North Carolina counties reappraise real estate on a 4- to 8-year cycle (Mecklenburg and Wake counties every 4 years), keeping property tax escrow bills predictable between revaluations.

Real $500,000 Monthly Payment Breakdown in North Carolina

On a $500,000 home in Charlotte or Raleigh at 6.5% APR with 20% down, property taxes average just ~$304/month ($3,650/year) and insurance averages ~$171/month—keeping total monthly PITI near ~$3,003/month, or ~$3,563/month with 10% down including PMI. Remember that coastal North Carolina counties (Wilmington, Outer Banks) carry higher wind/hail insurance premiums than Piedmont metros like Raleigh or Greensboro.

Frequently Asked Questions: North Carolina Mortgage & Property Taxes

How much are property taxes on a $500,000 home in North Carolina in 2026?

At the 0.73% statewide average, property taxes run roughly $3,650/year (~$304/month), ranging from ~$3,200 to $4,500 depending on city + county combined rates.

What is the monthly payment on a $500,000 NC home at 6.5% APR?

With 20% down, expect roughly $3,003/month ($2,528 P&I + $304 property tax + $171 insurance). With 10% down including PMI, expect ~$3,563/month.

Does North Carolina have a statewide Homestead Exemption for all homeowners?

North Carolina's Homestead Exclusion is targeted to qualifying seniors (age 65+ with low income) and disabled veterans, though its baseline 0.73% rate is already among the lowest in the East.
